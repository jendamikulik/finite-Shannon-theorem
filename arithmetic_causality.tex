\begin{abstract}
For a fixed finite seed law, we characterize exactly how much probability can be carried by policy-transcript tuples with no common causal realization. The answer is the total weight of atoms participating in subset-sum collisions; under uniform marginal error $\varepsilon$, collisions are replaced by subset sums at distance at most $2\varepsilon$. The upper bound is attained by a finite depth-two controlled tree. Consequently distinct subset sums are equivalent to universal exact causal rigidity, and half the minimum subset-sum separation is the sharp uniform robustness threshold. These statements do not use the finite Shannon bound. This note is not Lean-verified; literature priority has not been established.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions and quantifiers}
Let $S\in[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ have fixed positive weights $w_i$, with $\sum_iw_i=1$. For $A\subseteq[m]$ write $w(A)=\sum_{i\in A}w_i$. A finite controlled full-history tree $T$ has positive transition probabilities, finite nonempty action sets and finite nonempty output alphabets. Singleton output alphabets are allowed. Let $\Pi_T$ be its complete set of deterministic policies, and $L_\pi$ the terminal histories consistent with policy $\pi$.

A family of arbitrary deterministic decoders is a collection
\[
 f_\pi:[m]\longrightarrow L_\pi,\qquad\pi\in\Pi_T.
\]
No consistency across policies is assumed. If $P_\pi$ is the target transcript law generated by $T$, call the family $\varepsilon$-accurate when
\[
 \max_{\pi\in\Pi_T}\TV((f_\pi)_*w,P_\pi)\le\varepsilon,
\]
using $\TV(P,Q)=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|$. An index $i$ is bad when the tuple $(f_\pi(i))_\pi$ is not generated by a single causal response strategy on $T$. Write $\operatorname{Bad}(f)$ for these indices and $\eta(f)=w(\operatorname{Bad}(f))$.

For $\varepsilon\ge0$ define
\[
 R_\varepsilon(w)=
 \bigcup_{\substack{A,B\subseteq[m],\ A\ne B\\|w(A)-w(B)|\le2\varepsilon}}(A\mathbin\triangle B),
 \qquad r_\varepsilon(w)=w(R_\varepsilon(w)).
\]
The union can be empty. This definition depends only on the seed law, not on the tree or its depth. Equivalently, $i\in R_\varepsilon(w)$ if and only if some $c\in\{-1,0,1\}^m$ has $c_i\ne0$ and $|\sum_jc_jw_j|\le2\varepsilon$.

\section{Local conflicts characterize incompatibility}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local}
A tuple of policy-consistent terminal histories is realizable by one response strategy if and only if any two of its histories visiting the same history-action pair prescribe the same next output there.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Necessity follows because a response strategy assigns a single output to each history-action pair. Conversely, prescribe every output appearing in any of the histories. The hypothesis makes this prescription single-valued. Complete the remaining history-action pairs arbitrarily, using nonempty output alphabets. Induction along each policy history shows that the resulting strategy produces that history: the policy supplies its prescribed next action and the strategy supplies the prescribed output. Finiteness terminates the induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp arithmetic defect profile]\label{thm:profile}
For any fixed $w$ and $\varepsilon\ge0$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every $\varepsilon$-accurate decoder family on every finite controlled tree satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Bad}(f)\subseteq R_\varepsilon(w),\qquad
 \eta(f)\le r_\varepsilon(w).
\]
\item There exists a finite controlled tree of depth at most two and an $\varepsilon$-accurate decoder family with
\[
 \operatorname{Bad}(f)=R_\varepsilon(w).
\]
\end{enumerate}
In particular the maximum possible incompatible mass, over all such trees and families using this fixed seed law, equals $r_\varepsilon(w)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Upper bound]
Let $i$ be bad. By Lemma~\ref{lem:local}, there exist policies $\pi,\rho$ whose decoded histories at index $i$ both visit a history $v$, select the same action $a$ there, and produce distinct outputs $y\ne z$. Both policies therefore agree with the actions on the prefix $vay$ (up to and including action $a$). Set
\[
 A=\{j:f_\pi(j)\text{ has prefix }vay\},\quad
 B=\{j:f_\rho(j)\text{ has prefix }vay\}.
\]
Then $i\in A\setminus B$. Under either target policy law the probability of this prefix is the same history mass $p(vay)$. Total variation controls every prefix event, so
\[
 |w(A)-p(vay)|\le\varepsilon,\qquad
 |w(B)-p(vay)|\le\varepsilon.
\]
Thus $|w(A)-w(B)|\le2\varepsilon$ and $i\in R_\varepsilon(w)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Attainment]
If $R_\varepsilon(w)$ is empty, use a deterministic tree and a constant family. Otherwise select finitely many witness pairs $(A_j,B_j)$ whose symmetric differences cover $R_\varepsilon(w)$. One pair per index suffices, so there need be at most $m$ pairs. Each pair has distinct subsets and $|w(A_j)-w(B_j)|\le2\varepsilon$.

At the root offer an action for each $j$, with binary output $Y$ and target probability
\[
 \Pr(Y=0\mid j)=q_j:=\tfrac12(w(A_j)+w(B_j)).
\]
Positivity of every weight and $A_j\ne B_j$ imply $0<q_j<1$. Following $Y=0$, offer two actions $a,b$, both producing a deterministic terminal output. Following $Y=1$, stop.

For every policy selecting root action $j$, its choice at the hypothetical $Y=0$ node is either $a$ or $b$. In the former case the decoder outputs $Y=0$ precisely on $A_j$; in the latter precisely on $B_j$. It then follows the policy and produces the singleton terminal output if needed. Its transcript is policy-consistent. Its total variation error is respectively
\[
 |w(A_j)-q_j|=|w(B_j)-q_j|
 =\tfrac12|w(A_j)-w(B_j)|\le\varepsilon.
\]
For $i\in A_j\triangle B_j$, the two policies selecting $j$ but differing in their later action demand opposite outputs at the same root history-action pair. Thus $i$ is bad. If $i$ lies in no selected symmetric difference, its root response for every $j$ is independent of the later policy choice. These responses, followed by the singleton terminal outputs, form a realizing strategy. Hence the bad set is exactly the desired union.

The construction is stated for a labeled seed and need not distinguish indices having identical decoder tuples. If distinct joint-coupling atoms with the original weights are required, add one root action revealing $i$ with target law $w$. It is decoded identically under every policy and introduces no additional inconsistency or error; it makes the tuple map injective. Depth remains at most two.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact rigidity and the sharp noise threshold}
Put
\[
 \gamma(w)=\min_{A\ne B}|w(A)-w(B)|.
\]
The minimum exists; it can be zero.
\begin{corollary}[Universal exact rigidity]
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item All subset sums of $w$ are distinct.
\item Every exact decoder family using seed law $w$, on every finite controlled tree, is realizable by one causal strategy for each seed value, without changing the decoders or weights.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Distinct subset sums are equivalent to $R_0(w)=\varnothing$. Apply both directions of Theorem~\ref{thm:profile} with $\varepsilon=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp uniform robustness threshold]
Every $\varepsilon$-accurate family on every finite controlled tree using seed $w$ is compatible if and only if $2\varepsilon<\gamma(w)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If the strict inequality holds, $R_\varepsilon(w)$ is empty. If it fails, a minimizing pair in the definition of $\gamma(w)$ has nonempty symmetric difference contained in $R_\varepsilon(w)$, and the attaining construction gives a bad family. This also handles $\gamma(w)=\varepsilon=0$.
\end{proof}

For fixed $m$, the exact exceptional seed laws lie in the finite union of hyperplanes
\[
 \bigcup_{c\in\{-1,0,1\}^m\setminus\{0\}}
 \{w>0:\ \sum_iw_i=1,\ c\cdot w=0\}.
\]
Every nonempty intersection here is a proper hyperplane in the probability simplex. Outside this set, any exact family that exists is automatically compatible. This is a statement about seed weights, not about generic target kernels: perturbing seed weights generally destroys the prescribed marginals, and the result does not assert $C(T)=M(T)$ for generic trees.

\section{Examples and consequences}
For $w=(3/5,1/5,1/5)$, the only colliding symmetric differences involve indices $2,3$. Consequently $r_0(w)=2/5$: in every exact family at least $3/5$ of the seed mass is necessarily compatible, and this guarantee is attained at depth two.

For $w=(4,2,1)/7$, every subset sum is distinct, $r_0(w)=0$, and $\gamma(w)=1/7$. All families with uniform marginal total variation error strictly below $1/14$ are compatible. At $1/14$ there is already an incompatible family.

For the earlier separation witness $w=(3,2,2,1,1)/9$, every index participates in an exact collision, so $r_0(w)=1$. This is consistent with, but does not itself prove, its positive entropy advantage.

If $w$ is the positive atom vector of an ordinary entropy-minimizing coupling and its subset sums are distinct, then $C(T)=M(T)$. Conversely, a positive compatibility gap forces every ordinary entropy minimizer to have a nontrivial subset-sum collision. More quantitatively, combining the exact profile bound with the separately established repair theorem yields
\[
 C(T)-H(\Gamma)\le c_*\,r_0(w).
\]
The arithmetic theorem alone does not imply a positive entropy gap whenever a collision exists: the sharpness constructions can have $C(T)=M(T)$. Incompatibility of a particular coupling and the optimization gap are different questions.

\section{Verification status and scope}
The proofs above are finite elementary arguments. The accompanying Fraction-based Python program checks the attaining construction for several seed laws and errors and independently exhausts small two-policy decoder families. It is not a proof-assistant certificate and does not replace the universal proof. No Lean installation or repository modification was performed.

The repository consulted was \url{https://github.com/jendamikulik/finite-Shannon-theorem}, read on 8 October 2026. Its current README describes finite Shannon realization, truncation, and the exact entropy penalty; this arithmetic characterization is a separate proposed addition. Distinct subset sums are a classical notion. No claim is made that their use or this specific characterization has established publication priority. The universal extremal entropy gap $G_*$ is not evaluated here.
