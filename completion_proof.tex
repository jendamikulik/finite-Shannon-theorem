\section{Definitions and the inherited inputs}
Let $T$ be a locally finite controlled history tree, with a finite nonempty
set of actions at each history and a finite nonempty output set for each
action. Kernels have positive probabilities; zero-probability outputs can be
removed. Terminal histories are padded deterministically. Let $p(h)$ be the
product of the transition probabilities along a valid history $h$, and zero
on invalid raw histories. Thus $p(\varnothing)=1$ and
$\sum_y p(hay)=p(h)$ for every available action $a$.

A deterministic strategy gives an output at every history--action pair.
Its space $\cS$ is a countable product of finite discrete spaces, hence compact
and metrizable. Let $E_h$ be the clopen set of strategies agreeing with $h$.
An exact causal law is a Borel probability $\mu$ on $\cS$ satisfying
$\mu(E_h)=p(h)$ for every $h$.

For the transcript distribution $P_{n,\pi}$ of a deterministic policy at
depth $n$, set
\[
 A_K(P)=\max_{|D|\le K}P(D),\qquad
 a_K=\inf_{n,\pi}A_K(P_{n,\pi}),\qquad q=\sup_K a_K\in[0,1].
\]
An ordinary source has an exact transcript readout for each $(n,\pi)$,
without any compatibility requirement between the readouts.

The repository's finite extraction and diagonal construction give weights
$w_i\ge0$ and strategies $s_i\in\cS$, $i\in\mathbb N$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:input}
 \sum_iw_i=q,\qquad c(h):=\sum_iw_i\mathbf1_{E_h}(s_i)\le p(h).
\end{equation}
In its notation these are \texttt{diagMass\_eq\_tsum\_atomQ} and
\texttt{cylMass\_le\_path}. Also $r(h):=p(h)-c(h)\ge0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flow}
 r(\varnothing)=1-q,\qquad \sum_y r(hay)=r(h).
\end{equation}
These are the only inherited nontrivial inputs to the atomic completion.
The entropy argument in Section~\ref{sec:entropy} does not require this
particular greedy sequence.

\section{The missing measure and the sharp converse}
\begin{theorem}[Attained atomic capacity]\label{th:atomic}
The maximal atomic mass of an exact causal source and that of an ordinary
source are both $q$. In particular, an exact countable causal seed exists
if and only if $q=1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Define $\nu=\sum_iw_i\delta_{s_i}$. Countable additivity and
\eqref{eq:input} give $\nu(\cS)=q$ and $\nu(E_h)=c(h)$; repeated strategies
simply accumulate their weights at the same point.

For every history--action coordinate, use the probability distribution
\[
 k^r(y\mid h,a)=
 \begin{cases}
 r(hay)/r(h),&r(h)>0,\\
 k(y\mid h,a),&r(h)=0,
 \end{cases}
\]
where $k$ is the original output kernel. Equation~\eqref{eq:flow} proves
normalization in the first case; the second is already normalized. Take
the independent product $\lambda$ of these coordinate laws on $\cS$.
A valid path constrains distinct coordinates, so $\lambda(E_h)$ is the
product of its successive $k^r$ factors. Induction on its length gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tel}
 (1-q)\lambda(E_h)=r(h).
\end{equation}
Indeed, if the parent residual is positive the next factor is the required
ratio. If it is zero, every child residual is zero by nonnegativity and
\eqref{eq:flow}, and both sides remain zero. This also covers $q=1$.
Invalid histories have an empty cylinder and zero path mass. Therefore
\[
 \mu:=\nu+(1-q)\lambda
 \quad\text{satisfies}\quad
 \mu(\cS)=1,\qquad \mu(E_h)=c(h)+r(h)=p(h).
\]
Since $\mu\ge\nu$, its atomic mass is at least $q$.

For the converse, let $\eta$ be any ordinary source law with measurable
singletons and exact transcript readouts. A finite set $F$ of $K$ source
points maps, under each readout, into at most $K$ transcripts. Hence
\[
 \eta(F)\le A_K(P_{n,\pi})\quad\text{for every }n,\pi,
 \qquad \eta(F)\le a_K\le q.
\]
Taking the supremum over finite $F$ gives
$\At(\eta):=\sum_x\eta(\{x\})\le q$. This applies to $\mu$ as well,
so $\At(\mu)=q$.

A source and its full tuple of transcript readouts yield the same maximum:
the pushforward tuple law has atomic mass at least that of the source,
and the preceding upper bound also applies to the tuple law. Thus the
ordinary-coupling maximum is attained too. If $q=1$, $\nu$ alone is an
exact probability supported on the countable set $\{s_i:i\in\mathbb N\}$.
Conversely, a countable exact probability has atomic mass one, so the upper
bound forces $q=1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Entropy: no escape of mass}\label{sec:entropy}
Use natural logarithms in this section. For a countable seed with weights
$v_i$, set $H(v)=\sum_i-v_i\ln v_i\in[0,\infty]$, with $0\ln0=0$.
Let $C_n$ be the infimum of seed entropy among sources exact through depth
$n$, and $C_\infty$ the infimum among seeds exact at every finite depth.
The infimum of an empty class is $+\infty$.

We may enumerate positive masses in decreasing order and pad a finite
list with zeros. Here is why this is legitimate even for an infinite
support. Every nonempty remainder of a summable sequence of positive
masses has a largest element: its positive supremum is attained among
the finitely many entries exceeding half that supremum. Successive
selection of a largest entry exhausts the support, since only finitely
many masses exceed any fixed positive threshold. Relabeling and deleting
zero-mass labels preserve entropy and all readouts.

At a finite horizon these definitions agree with optimization over a
finite distribution on finite strategies. Restriction to depth $n$ maps
any seed into the finite set of depth-$n$ strategies. Merging equal
strategies cannot increase entropy: for a group of masses of total $p$,
\[
 \sum_j-v_j\ln v_j=-p\ln p+
       p\sum_j-\frac{v_j}{p}\ln\frac{v_j}{p}\ge -p\ln p
\]
when $p>0$, and both sides are zero when $p=0$. Conversely, any finite
strategy extends by arbitrary outputs beyond depth $n$. Thus these are
the original finite-horizon costs, not a stronger feasibility requirement.
The Lean file uses the ranked, zero-padded model as its explicit definition;
the preceding relabeling and finite-quotient identifications are supplied
here as mathematical arguments, not as additional compiled declarations.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform tail bound]\label{lem:tail}
If $v_0\ge v_1\ge\cdots\ge0$, $\sum_iv_i=1$, and $H(v)\le U<\infty$,
then for every integer $K\ge1$,
\[
 \sum_{i\ge K}v_i\le\frac{U}{\ln(K+1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$(i+1)v_i\le\sum_{j=0}^iv_j\le1$. Hence, for $i\ge K$ with $v_i>0$,
$-\ln v_i\ge\ln(K+1)$. Multiply by $v_i$ and sum. Zero terms cause
no difficulty. All entropy terms are nonnegative.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact compactness with an entropy bound]\label{lem:compact}
Suppose seed $j$ has ranked weights $v_i^{(j)}$ and strategy labels
$s_i^{(j)}$, is exact through depth $j$, and
$H(v^{(j)})\le b_j$, where $b_j\to B<\infty$ and $b_j\le U<\infty$.
Then there is a seed exact at all depths with entropy at most $B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The space $[0,1]^{\mathbb N}\times\cS^{\mathbb N}$ is compact metrizable.
Pass to a subsequence $j_m\to\infty$ on which, for each label $i$,
$v_i^{(j_m)}\to v_i$ and $s_i^{(j_m)}\to s_i$. The limiting weights
remain nonnegative and decreasing. Each finite head has mass at most one,
so $\sum_iv_i\le1$. Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} gives, for every fixed $K\ge1$,
\[
 1-\sum_{i<K}v_i
   =\lim_m\left(1-\sum_{i<K}v_i^{(j_m)}\right)
   \le\frac{U}{\ln(K+1)}.
\]
Letting $K\to\infty$ proves $\sum_iv_i=1$. This is the essential
no-escape step; coordinatewise convergence alone would not suffice.

Fix a history $h$. Its cylinder is clopen, so for each fixed $i$ the
indicators $\mathbf1_{E_h}(s_i^{(j_m)})$ eventually equal
$\mathbf1_{E_h}(s_i)$. For every $K$, exactness at depth $j_m\ge|h|$ gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{i<K}v_i^{(j_m)}\mathbf1_{E_h}(s_i^{(j_m)})&\le p(h),\\
 \sum_{i<K}v_i^{(j_m)}(1-\mathbf1_{E_h}(s_i^{(j_m)}))&\le1-p(h).
\end{align*}
Pass first to $m\to\infty$, then to $K\to\infty$. The two limiting
series sum to one; their upper bounds also sum to one. Both inequalities
are therefore equalities. In particular,
$\sum_i v_i\mathbf1_{E_h}(s_i)=p(h)$ for every $h$.

Finally, $x\mapsto-x\ln x$ is continuous on $[0,1]$. For each $K$,
\[
 \sum_{i<K}-v_i\ln v_i
 =\lim_m\sum_{i<K}-v_i^{(j_m)}\ln v_i^{(j_m)}
 \le\lim_m b_{j_m}=B.
\]
Taking the supremum over $K$ proves $H(v)\le B$. No continuity of entropy
on the full countable simplex has been used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Projective entropy identity and attainment]\label{th:entropy}
For every locally finite controlled tree,
\[
 \boxed{C_\infty=\sup_{n\ge0}C_n.}
\]
If this value is finite, one exact countable seed attains it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Restriction gives $C_n\le C_{n+1}\le C_\infty$. This proves the result
if $\sup_nC_n=+\infty$. Otherwise put $B=\sup_nC_n<\infty$.
By the definition of infimum, choose a depth-$n$ exact seed with
\[
 H(v^{(n)})<C_n+\frac1{n+1}\le B+\frac1{n+1}.
\]
There is no need to assume existence of finite-horizon minimizers.
Extend each finite strategy if necessary and rank its weights.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:compact} with $b_n=B+1/(n+1)$ and $U=B+1$.
The resulting infinite seed has entropy at most $B$. Restriction supplies
the opposite inequality, proving both equality and attainment.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Connection to the finite Shannon bound]
Return to bits and let $L_n$ denote the finite adaptive-envelope entropy.
If the finite theorem gives
$L_n\le C_n\le L_n+c_*$ with $c_*=(1+\log_2 e)/2$, then
\[
 L_\infty\le C_\infty\le L_\infty+c_*,\qquad
 L_\infty:=\sup_n L_n.
\]
In particular, $L_\infty<\infty$ if and only if a finite-entropy exact
seed exists on the whole tree. This condition implies $q=1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Changing logarithm base multiplies all entropy costs by $1/\ln2$.
Take suprema in the finite bounds and use Theorem~\ref{th:entropy}.
Attainment provides the seed when the bound is finite. Conversely, any
finite-entropy exact seed bounds every $L_n$ by restriction. Its countable
support and Theorem~\ref{th:atomic} imply $q=1$.
\end{proof}

\section{What is included in the formalization}
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{MeasureCompletion.lean}: residual product measure, exact
mixture, cylinder probabilities and probability normalization.
\item \texttt{AtomicCapacity.lean}: arbitrary-source upper bound,
attainment, and the countable-seed equivalence $q=1$.
\item \texttt{EntropyLimit.lean}: ranked-seed definitions, tail estimate,
compactness, projective entropy identity, attainment, and the exact
probability measure of the optimal seed. Entropy is in nats in this file.
\end{itemize}
The explicit Lean costs optimize over ranked countable seeds. The proof
of their identification with differently encoded finite optimization
problems is given above in prose. The envelope corollary combines the
projective identity with the repository's finite theorem; it is not an
additional Lean declaration in this package. None of these new files has
been compiled here. Successful compilation and inspection of
\texttt{\#print axioms} remain required before claiming a formal certificate.
