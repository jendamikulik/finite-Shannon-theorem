\begin{abstract}
We study how prescribed observable laws admit a single deterministic realization
driven by a random seed, uniformly over adaptive choices. Exact reconstruction
under one admissible policy fixes the optimal seed entropy. We apply this
principle to sum-and-moment memory and to sequential observation of Fourier
sectors. A constant or vanishing readout can conceal arbitrarily large finite
entropy, while access to a reconstructing sequence of readouts recovers its exact
simulation cost. We also prove an exact reduction of almost-surely safe-policy
simulation to a viable subtree. These elementary results organize a common
framework; no historical priority is claimed. The title denotes unfolding an
interaction history, not a derivation of cosmology. A companion causal Shannon
bound is stated conditionally on its hypotheses and is not reproved here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and the distinction that matters}
Three objects must be kept separate: a state or seed, the laws it realizes, and
the interface through which it is observed. Equal observable laws need not mean
equal hidden states. A zero readout need not mean zero information in a different
readout. Conversely, hidden structure cannot be inferred merely because an
output is zero.

Our main results are finite probabilistic statements. Signal labels may belong
to an infinite-dimensional function space, but every random signal considered
below has finite support. Thus its Shannon entropy is discrete entropy, not
differential entropy, signal energy, thermodynamic entropy, or description length
of an arbitrary real number. Exact arithmetic and computation time are separate
resources.

\section{A single seed for an adaptive interface}
Let $T$ be a finite tree of complete histories. At a nonterminal history $h$,
an experimenter chooses $a\in A(h)$ from a finite nonempty set and observes
$y\in Y(h,a)$ from a finite nonempty set, with probability $p(y\mid h,a)$.
These probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one. A deterministic policy
$\pi$ selects actions using only the observed history. It does not inspect the
hidden seed.

\begin{definition}[Exact realization]
A finite seed $S\sim\mu$ and a deterministic decoder $f$ realize a class
$\Pi$ of policies if
\[
 A_t=\pi(H_{t-1}),\quad Y_t=f(S,H_{t-1},A_t),\quad
 H_t=H_{t-1}\mathbin{\|}(A_t,Y_t),\quad H_0=\varnothing,
\]
induce the prescribed transcript law for every $\pi\in\Pi$.
The law $\mu$ and the decoder $f$ are fixed independently of the selected policy.
Write
\[
 C_\Pi(T)=\inf_{(\mu,f)\,\text{exact for }\Pi}\HH(S),
 \qquad C(T)=C_{\text{all deterministic policies}}(T).
\]
\end{definition}
The shared decoder enforces agreement at shared histories. The quantifiers are
$\exists(\mu,f)\ \forall\pi$, not a separate simulator chosen for each policy.
A finite realization always exists: pre-sample independently one output for every
history--action pair. Along any trajectory, the next table entry is fresh
relative to the entries determining the past. This reproduces the conditional
laws by induction.

In the language of the companion project~\cite{repo}, let $I_\pi$ denote
the transcript information $-\log_2P_T^\pi(H_n)$ on positive-mass transcripts.
The lower CDF envelope is
\[
 G_T(u)=\min_\pi\Pr(I_\pi\le u),\qquad
 L(T)=\int_0^\infty[1-G_T(u)]\,du.
\]
The companion finite causal Shannon theorem states, on its applicable class,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sandwich}
 L(T)\le C(T)\le L(T)+\cstar,
 \qquad \cstar=\frac{1+\log_2e}{2}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:sandwich} is an imported result, not a premise needed for
the direct identities proved below. The latter allow zero probabilities.
No general infinite-horizon extension is established in this paper.

\section{Exact reconstruction and observational compression}
For a finite-valued random variable $X$ and deterministic $q$, the chain rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
 \HH(X)=\HH(q(X))+\HH(X\mid q(X)).
\end{equation}
Hence $q$ preserves entropy precisely when $X$ is recoverable from $q(X)$ almost
surely. The decoder can be defined arbitrarily on values outside the support.
A nonzero output alone does not guarantee recoverability.

\begin{theorem}[Reconstructing-policy principle]\label{thm:reconstruct}
Suppose a finite-support random variable $Z$ drives a deterministic causal
interface exact for all $\pi\in\Pi$. If one policy $\pi_0$ produces a transcript
$X$ from which $Z$ is almost surely recoverable, then
\[
 \boxed{C_\Pi=\HH(Z).}
\]
The minimum is attained by the seed $S=Z$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The given interface with seed $Z$ gives the upper bound. Since $X$ is a function
of $Z$ and conversely $Z$ is recoverable from $X$, their entropies agree.
Under $\pi_0$, every competing exact simulator produces a transcript $X'$ with
the same law as $X$, deterministically from its seed $U$. Thus
$\HH(U)\ge\HH(X')=\HH(X)=\HH(Z)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Restriction and recoding]
If $\Pi_1\subseteq\Pi_2$, then $C_{\Pi_1}\le C_{\Pi_2}$.
Invertible relabelings of actions and outputs that respect histories preserve
the optimal seed cost.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An exact simulator for the larger class also serves the smaller one.
For an invertible relabeling, translate policies and decoder outputs in each
direction. This gives simulators of identical seed entropy in both directions.
\end{proof}
An unused independent seed component changes no observable law, while increasing
seed entropy. Therefore the hidden realization is generally not identifiable;
the optimum is an operational resource quantity.

\section{Memory that survives cancellation}
Fix real addresses $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ and complex deposits
$Z=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)$ with an arbitrary finite-support joint law. Define
\[
 S_0=M_0=0,\qquad S_t=S_{t-1}+Z_t,\qquad
 M_t=M_{t-1}+x_tZ_t.
\]
At time $t$ the observer may request $S_t$ or $M_t$. Observation is passive:
it does not change the deposits or their law.

\begin{corollary}[Observable writer]
The optimal common seed entropy is
\[
 C_n=\HH(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)
 =\sum_{t=1}^n\HH(Z_t\mid Z_1,\ldots,Z_{t-1}).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Always requesting the sum reconstructs $Z_t=S_t-S_{t-1}$. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:reconstruct} and the chain rule.
\end{proof}
The update algebra is the writer used in the supplied ONE manuscript
\cite{one}; the probability law and observation interface are additional inputs.
This use of the writer is not an endorsement of the manuscript's cosmological
claims.

For two deposits $Z_1=W$, $Z_2=-W$ and $x_1\ne x_2$,
\[
 S_2=0,\qquad M_2=(x_1-x_2)W.
\]
With terminal-only observation, the sum interface has cost zero, whereas the
interface offering either sum or moment has cost $\HH(W)$. A constant seed
suffices for the first; seed $W$ suffices for the second and its injective moment
readout gives the matching lower bound. This is not true of the sum interface
if the earlier sum $S_1=W$ is also observable.

\section{Fourier sectors as an exact observation interface}
Use the Fourier convention
\[
 (\F f)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx.
\]
The integral is first defined on Schwartz functions; Plancherel's theorem
extends it unitarily to $L^2(\mathbb R)$. Let $h_n$ be the normalized Hermite
basis in this convention, so $h_0(x)=2^{1/4}e^{-\pi x^2}$ and
\[
 \F h_n=(-i)^nh_n,\qquad \F^4=I.
\]
These are classical facts, not new consequences of the causal seed theorem.
Euler's identity alone is not a proof of them.

For $\Lambda=\{1,-i,-1,i\}$ define
\[
 P_\lambda=\frac14\sum_{j=0}^3\lambda^{-j}\F^j.
\]
Applying this polynomial to $h_n$ gives $h_n$ if $(-i)^n=\lambda$ and zero
otherwise, by the finite geometric-sum identity. Completeness of the Hermite
basis consequently yields
\[
 P_\lambda P_\nu=\mathbf1_{\lambda=\nu}P_\lambda,
 \quad P_\lambda^*=P_\lambda,\quad
 \sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}P_\lambda=I.
\]
In particular, $P_1$ is a projection onto the Fourier-fixed subspace, not a
universal lossless representation. For example $P_1h_1=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Sequential Fourier-sector reconstruction]\label{thm:sectors}
Let $Z$ be an $L^2(\mathbb R)$-valued random signal with finite support. In a
four-round passive interface, each action requests one of the outputs
$P_\lambda Z$, and repeated requests are allowed. The optimal seed entropy
for all adaptive policies is exactly $\HH(Z)$.
If only $P_1$ is available, the optimal seed entropy is $\HH(P_1Z)$.
The difference is
\[
 \boxed{\HH(Z)-\HH(P_1Z)=\HH(Z\mid P_1Z).}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The fixed policy requesting each of the four sectors recovers
$Z=\sum_\lambda P_\lambda Z$. The seed $Z$ supplies every adaptive sequence
of requests. Theorem~\ref{thm:reconstruct} proves the first claim. If only
$P_1$ is available, the first output has entropy $\HH(P_1Z)$ and all repeated
outputs equal it. Taking this output itself as seed proves the second claim.
Equation~\eqref{eq:loss} gives the difference.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A fixed readout with arbitrarily large accessible entropy]
Let $W$ be a finite real random variable and $Z=h_0+Wh_1$. Then
\[
 P_1Z=h_0,\qquad P_{-i}Z=Wh_1.
\]
The fixed-sector interface costs zero; the interface allowing either of these
two sectors costs $\HH(W)$, even in a single round.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first readout is constant; the second uniquely identifies $W$ because
$h_1\ne0$. Seed $W$ generates both readouts, and the second gives the lower bound.
\end{proof}
Uniform $W$ on $m$ values gives a gap of $\log_2m$. The hidden information
resides in $Z$, not in the constant output $h_0$.

\paragraph{Why sequential access is essential.}
Joint reconstructability of a list of queries is not sufficient if only one
query can ever be asked. Let $U,V$ be independent fair bits and
$Z=Uh_0+Vh_1$. One round allowing either sector query costs only one bit: a
single fair seed $B$ can answer $Bh_0$ or $Bh_1$ as requested. The separate
marginals are correct, though this seed does not reproduce their joint
independence. Two rounds allowing both queries require two bits, because
the fixed policy requesting both reconstructs $(U,V)$. This demonstrates why
the experimental interface, including its horizon, belongs in the theorem.

\section{Safety and the viable subtree}
In a finite controlled tree, let $B$ be a set of forbidden histories closed under
extension. Define $V(h)$ as the minimum probability of visiting $B$ before
termination in the continuation tree rooted at $h$. Backward induction gives
\[
 V(h)=\begin{cases}
 1,&h\in B,\\
 0,&h\notin B\text{ is terminal},\\
 \min_a\sum_y p(y\mid h,a)V(hay),&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Call $h$ viable if $V(h)=0$. At a viable nonterminal history retain
\[
 A_0(h)=\{a:\ p(y\mid h,a)>0\Rightarrow V(hay)=0\text{ for all }y\}.
\]
This set is nonempty. If the root is viable, let $T_0$ be the tree reached
using these actions and positive-probability edges. Let $\Pi_0$ contain all
policies of the original tree with zero failure probability.

\begin{theorem}[Exact safety restriction]
Under these assumptions,
\[
 \boxed{C_{\Pi_0}(T)=C(T_0).}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every policy on $T_0$ extends arbitrarily outside $T_0$ to a safe policy on $T$.
Restricting a simulator exact for all safe policies therefore gives a simulator
on $T_0$. If its decoder assigns a deleted output at an unused seed--history
pair, replace that output by any retained output: exactness guarantees the
replacement changes no positive-probability run of any restricted policy.
There are only finitely many policies and histories. Seed entropy is unchanged.
Thus $C(T_0)\le C_{\Pi_0}(T)$.

Conversely, extend a decoder on $T_0$ arbitrarily to the remaining interfaces of
$T$. Fix a safe policy $\pi$. Every history it reaches with positive probability
has a zero-risk continuation. Hence at each such nonterminal history its action
lies in $A_0(h)$. Define a policy on $T_0$ agreeing there with $\pi$, and choose
arbitrary retained actions elsewhere. Induction on the horizon shows that its
target law equals that of $\pi$. Run both decoders with the same seed. They
agree until a first departure from those histories; exactness of the restricted
run makes the probability of such a departure zero. The extension is therefore
exact for $\pi$. Since this holds for every safe policy, taking infima gives
the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}
This is an operational restriction, not a change to the full original laws.
For example, a one-step tree with a deterministic safe action and a second
action uniform on $m$ forbidden outcomes has $C(T)=\log_2m$ but
$C_{\Pi_0}(T)=0$. If the root is not viable, no safe policy exists.
When the companion bound applies to $T_0$, it implies
$L(T_0)\le C_{\Pi_0}(T)\le L(T_0)+\cstar$.

\section{Invariants, quantum measurements, and the physical boundary}
For a smooth controlled system $\dot x=F_a(x)$, a differentiable quantity $U$
is conserved along every fixed-action trajectory when
$dU_x(F_a(x))=0$ for every action and state. An intervention $\Theta_a$
preserves that quantity only when $U\circ\Theta_a=U$ is separately established.
Commuting with the flow is insufficient: scaling the harmonic oscillator state
by two commutes with its flow but multiplies $q^2+p^2$ by four. Such conservation
identities alone do not specify a probability law or a seed entropy.

For quantum experiments, a precise bridge can be specified at the level of
instruments. Let $\rho_h$ be the conditional density operator and let
$\{\mathcal I_{a,y}\}_y$ be completely positive trace-nonincreasing maps
whose sum is trace preserving. Then
\[
 p(y\mid h,a)=\mathrm{tr}\,\mathcal I_{a,y}(\rho_h),\qquad
 \rho_{hay}=\frac{\mathcal I_{a,y}(\rho_h)}{p(y\mid h,a)}
 \quad\text{when }p(y\mid h,a)>0.
\]
Finite action and outcome alphabets and a finite horizon produce a finite
controlled tree. State updates incorporate measurement back-action; a passive
writer does not automatically implement them. Probabilities on unreachable
zero-probability histories require a specified convention if they are retained.

For a normalized two-path joint state
$|\Psi\rangle=|\psi_1\rangle|d_1\rangle+|\psi_2\rangle|d_2\rangle$,
with normalized detector states, tracing out the detector gives
\[
 p(x)=|\psi_1(x)|^2+|\psi_2(x)|^2+
 2\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\psi_1(x)\overline{\psi_2(x)}
 \langle d_2|d_1\rangle\right).
\]
This is standard quantum mechanics~\cite{feynman,nc}, not a consequence of
Shannon entropy. A finite detector partitions positions into recorded outcomes.
Exact simulation refers to those outcome laws, not automatically to a complete
continuous quantum field.

The defensible direction is therefore
\[
 \text{physical model and experimental protocol}
 \ \longrightarrow\ p(y\mid h,a)\ \longrightarrow\ T
 \ \longrightarrow\ C(T).
\]
A displayed Lagrangian alone omits the preparation and measurement specification.
No Lagrangian, Born rule, gravitational dynamics, or consciousness mechanism is
derived here. Classical centralized simulation of a temporal interface does not
establish spatially local hidden variables or efficient computation.

\section{A conditional estimate is not a proof of RH}
The supplied Fourier manuscript also proposes a conditional bound of the form
\[
 \frac{Q(\tau,L)-E_0L}{L}\ge A\tau^4-B\tau^3-D,
 \qquad A>0,\quad B,D\ge0,
\]
valid uniformly for $L\ge1$ and $\tau\ge\tau_0$. Define
\[
 \tau_\star=\max\left\{\tau_0,1,\frac{2B}{A},
                         \left(\frac{4D}{A}\right)^{1/4}\right\}.
\]
Then for $\tau\ge\tau_\star$ the right side is strictly positive: it is at
least $A\tau^4/2-D$, which is at least $D>0$ if $D>0$, and is positive if
$D=0$. This is a valid elementary implication. It does not prove the assumed
bridge or quartic coercivity estimates. Fourier-sector decomposition and seed
entropy do not supply those estimates. No proof of the Riemann hypothesis is
claimed in this synthesis.

\section{Conclusions and the remaining mathematical question}
The precise content of ``unfolding'' is a sequence of choices and responses
whose complete history is generated by one common seed and decoder. Reconstruction
turns an entropy lower bound into an exact optimum. Fourier sectors give a
concrete family of observation interfaces, and safety gives a concrete
restriction of the policies to be supported.

The main operational identities established here are
\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 C_{\text{reconstructing interface}}&=\HH(Z),\\
 C_{\text{four sequential sectors}}-C_{\text{fixed sector}}
     &=\HH(Z\mid P_1Z),\\
 C_{\text{safe policies}}(T)&=C(T_0).
 \end{aligned}}
\]
Each equality has its stated finite-model assumptions. None equates information
with matter, probability with physical energy, or entropy with computational
time. In particular, no implication $P=NP$ follows. Love, intention, and
metaphysical potential can motivate a question but are not variables identified
by these proofs.

A concrete further problem is the cost of supporting all policies with failure
probability at most $\varepsilon>0$. This class can couple choices across
branches through a global risk budget, so a fixed local pruning rule need not
describe it. A sharp structural characterization and corresponding entropy
bounds remain research targets, not results of this paper.
